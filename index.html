Bukas, how it works

You write something and hold the button to seal it. Bukas keeps it and picks a day to give it back. That could be in two weeks or in four years. You don't get to choose, and you won't know when.

Until then you can't open it, search for it or read it again. There's no archive and nothing to scroll through. You just wait, and one day it turns up.

It's a place to write for no one, not even the person you'll be later. Nothing gets counted and nothing nags you to come back. When something does return, read it, and then decide if you want to seal it again.

Bukas is Tagalog for tomorrow. It also means open.

How it works

You write something, hold to seal it, and it’s gone. You can’t reread it, search it or browse it. At some point it comes back. Could be days, could be years, and you don’t get to pick. Sometimes two turn up together from different years with nothing to explain why. Once you’ve read it, you seal it again and it goes back in.

Why it works that way

Every other journalling app is built to help you get more out of what you wrote. Search, prompts, summaries, mood graphs, an AI that explains you to yourself. All of it assumes the problem is that you haven’t had enough insight yet. Usually that isn’t the problem. When you can go back whenever you want, you go back when you’re already in a certain mood, and you end up finding the entries that match it. Bukas takes that choice off you, so your own words turn up when you weren’t expecting them. That’s the only way they’re going to read differently.

There’s nothing here to finish. No streaks, no counts, no badges, nothing telling you how much is sealed. Nothing happens if you stop. Nothing gets measured either, because once something is measured it turns into a thing you’re supposed to improve, and that isn’t what this is.

Your writing stays yours

You can export everything whenever you want, sealed entries included. The lock is part of the app, not the file. Your entries sit against your account so they can find you again years from now, and nothing reads what’s inside them. The system only ever looks at dates.

What it’s built on

Camus argued the absurd isn’t a problem to be solved but a tension to be held: resolve it and you’ve stopped looking at it. Sartre’s point was that you’re answerable for your life whether or not you chose the conditions of it. Byung-Chul Han describes how we’ve come to exploit ourselves willingly, in the name of becoming our best selves. And John Vervaeke’s account of the meaning crisis argues that what’s gone wrong isn’t a lack of information but a breakdown in how we work out what matters — which is why more insight can’t fix it.

Leaving

Export first if you want to keep anything. Deleting your account removes it and everything in it, sealed entries included. It can’t be undone.
